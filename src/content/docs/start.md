This is the written half of [Konjam Coding](https://konjamcoding.me). The videos are on
YouTube, spoken in Tamil. These pages are the part you copy and paste from.

## Why both

A video is good at showing you that something is possible and what it looks like when it
works. It is bad at being a reference. Nobody wants to scrub back through eighteen minutes
to find one `apt install` line.

So each tutorial here is the same material as its video, written out: the commands, the
files, the output you should expect, and the errors you will probably hit. Watch the video
once, then keep the page open while you work.

Some pages have no video yet, and some videos have no page yet. Both are fine. They are
linked to each other where both exist.

## Which language is where

| | Language |
| --- | --- |
| Videos | Tamil |
| These pages | English |
| Code, commands, errors | English |
| About and contact on the main site | Tamil and English |

The written tutorials are in English for now. A wrong technical translation is worse than
no translation, and translating "transform tree" or "quality of service profile" badly
makes a hard subject harder. When there is money to pay a real translator, these pages get
a proper Tamil version. Machine translation is not going to be used for them.

## Versions

Every tutorial says what it was last tested against, right under the title. ROS 2
especially moves: a command that worked on Humble can be renamed or removed by the next
distro.

If the page says Jazzy and you are on Humble, expect at least one thing to differ. That is
not you being bad at this.

## When something is wrong

Tell someone. This repository is public and the fix is usually one line.

- **Comment at the bottom of the page.** Needs a GitHub account.
- **Comment on the video.** Needs nothing. Other people reading the comments usually have
  the same question.
- **Open an issue** on [KonjamCoding/learn](https://github.com/KonjamCoding/learn/issues).
- **Send the fix yourself.** Every page has an "Edit this page" link at the bottom that
  takes you straight to it on GitHub.

Corrections are the most useful thing anyone sends. A broken install command stops a
beginner completely, and the person who wrote it has usually moved on and cannot see it any
more.

## What this is not

Not a bootcamp, not a certificate, and not one-to-one mentoring. One person makes it, after
work. It is a map, not a guide standing next to you.
